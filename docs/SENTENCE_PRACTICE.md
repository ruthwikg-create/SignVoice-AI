# Words, alphabets and sentence practice — v1.3

Choose **Text → signs**, select a language, type words or a sentence, and choose **Show me the signs**. The planner matches the longest available word or phrase in that language, then displays a sequence with video, image, source-lesson or missing status. It never silently drops a missing word or replaces another language with ASL.

**Watch sequence** begins at the first step. Embedded Google Drive, YouTube and Vimeo demonstrations load inside the app. Providers may block embeds or autoplay; use their player controls or the separate source link. Select **Next sign** after watching a provider video. Your own local videos support slow playback, looping and optional advance when playback ends. Local images display inside the same player, but cannot demonstrate motion.

This is vocabulary practice in the input order. It does not translate written sentence grammar into grammatical signing, choose a contextually correct meaning automatically, or produce a fluent continuous signed sentence. Learn full sentence examples from the provider or a fluent teacher. International Sign is not a universal national sign language.

## Add a missing word

1. Follow the selected language's dictionary link and use its written lookup language. English text is not automatically translated into French, Chinese, Korean or any other written language.
2. Check the intended meaning, exact sign language and regional variant.
3. Open **Add a verified lesson for a word**, enter the word/phrase, source name and HTTPS source page. Add an optional YouTube, Vimeo or Google Drive video link and signing notes.
4. Confirm that you checked the language and meaning, then save. The link becomes available in typed practice and the searchable library on this device. Export your lesson links for backup or import them on another device.

For permitted local files, use **World languages → Personal lesson videos & images**. Labels must match the words you type. Videos and images are stored in your browser and are not uploaded. Keep original files as backups. Saving a lesson does not train recognition; use **Teach a sign** to record landmark examples separately.

## Alphabets

The app includes its original public-domain ASL chart and language-specific source guides where verified. Some guides are provider links rather than embedded videos. Missing alphabet guides are identified explicitly; the app does not invent handshapes or assume all languages use A–Z.

Optional spelling uses the bundled ASL chart for ASL. In other languages it only spells a missing Latin word when every needed letter has a saved or imported reference with `kind: "alphabet"` for that language. Use the manual-letter checkbox when saving a verified A–Z lesson. A whole alphabet tutorial is a guide, not a reference for each individual letter. Japanese kana and other scripts can be saved as ordinary lessons; their fingerspelling is not synthesized from Latin letters.

Chinese and Japanese input can use the browser's word segmentation when spaces are absent. Segmentation is a lookup aid, not linguistic translation, and may need manual spacing or corrected labels.

## Current coverage

All 23 distinct requested choices have profiles and source learning links, including separate Korean and Kenyan KSL. Auslan was listed twice and appears once. French is labelled LSF and Filipino is labelled FSL. NS is interpreted as Norwegian; TSL as Taiwan, CSL as Chinese and ZGS as Zimbabwean. Full names are visible to avoid ambiguous selections.

The bundled per-word indexes remain ASL and ISL. Other languages have alphabet/source guides where available, plus your saved links, imported indexes and local lessons. They do not have complete word/video/letter catalogs by default. Provider playback and each example's linguistic accuracy still need manual review. Some providers require subscription, registration or browser verification.
